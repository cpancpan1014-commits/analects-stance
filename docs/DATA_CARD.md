# Data card

What is in this repository, where it came from, what it is good for, and where
it will mislead you. Numbers here are generated by `scripts/build_release_corpus.py`
and `scripts/build_network.py`; `scripts/verify.py` checks them.

Version 1.0 · 2026-11-05 · companion to the ICISE-IE 2026 paper.

## 2. The corpus in numbers

| | chapters | characters |
|---|---|---|
| canonical text (經) | 501 | 21,989 |
| He Yan, *Collected Explanations* (魏) | 444 | 24,126 |
| Xing Bing, *Subcommentary* (北宋) | 470 | 147,107 |
| Zhu Xi, *Collected Annotations* (南宋) | 457 | 71,421 |
| Ito Jinsai, *Ancient Meaning* (江户前期) | 440 | 100,582 |
| Ogyu Sorai, *Riddle* (江户中期) | 418 | 159,206 |

20 books, 501 chapter units (the received division; chapter ids are `book-chapter`,
e.g. `01-01`). All five commentaries are present in 339 chapters; at least one is
present in 496. The corpus is not a critical edition: it is one transcription of
one printing per commentary, and the paper treats it as such.

Characters removed as OCR noise, by layer: He Yan 0, Xing Bing 0, Zhu Xi 56,
Jinsai 857, Sorai 1,326, canonical text 2,184. The two Japanese layers were
transcribed from page images and carry most of it: running heads (`論語古義 論語徵`),
volume marks (`卷一13`), stray page numbers, proofreading circles.

## 3. The edition defect, and what was done about it

In six chapters the "Jinsai" layer holds Sorai's text: `04-16`, `07-17`, `08-15`,
`12-21`, `12-22`, `15-14`. The printed edition binds the *Riddle* and the *Ancient
Meaning* in one volume and mis-assigns page ranges where chapters run short; in
`12-22` and `15-14` the Jinsai layer even contains 仁齋先生曰, which Jinsai could
not write of himself.

The judgement is stored in the data, not in code: those six rows carry
`kogi_status: "suppressed"`, and `data/contamination.json` records the similarity,
the quoted self-reference and the heads of both layers. Pipeline code reads the
field. Delete the field and the layer returns — that is deliberate, so a reader
can disagree with the call. Similarity runs 0.851–0.971 across the six; a seventh
chapter, `11-05`, scores 0.889 on a 24-character layer that shares one classical
phrase and is listed as a near miss.

Effect: 44 items and 7 edges withdrawn; Jinsai's rejection rate falls from 2.8%
to 2.1%. No other layer pair exceeds 0.85 similarity.

## 4. Gold standard

40 items, stratified: 14 rejections with a marker, 8 without, 12 assertions,
6 adoptions, drawn from chapters not used in prompt construction. Each was judged
with 300–400 characters of context on three questions: is this the term the
commentator expounds, is the stance right, is the target right.

38/40 stances correct (95.0%), Cohen's κ = .917. The two errors run one way, both
labelling a quotation as the commentator's own assertion: `10-04` (Zhu Xi reports
Lu Deming on a recension) and `04-26` (Xing Bing notes a pronunciation).

Three of the 40 are not in the cross-family subset — items 7, 20 and 36, whose He
Yan note is 9, 12 and 31 characters. The blind judging pool skips layers under 40
characters, so there was no context to judge. The subset is 37, not 40, and every
figure in Table IV is computed on those 37.

**One human annotator.** Every κ against gold is agreement with the person who
wrote the schema and the prompt. It bounds prompt fit, not truth. The one
human-independent check in the paper is model against model: GLM re-judged 200
sampled items without seeing the primary model's answers, and the two agree at
κ = .843 (95% CI .77–.90) on the 197 that parsed (`scripts/second_coder.py
report`). That is a stability estimate across model families, not a validation
against expert judgement — a second human annotator remains the largest gap, and
the first item of further work.

## 5. What this corpus is not

- **Not a critical edition.** Variants between printings are not recorded. Where
  the base text and a commentary disagree on a character, the commentary is as
  transcribed.
- **Not chapter-aligned for the two control layers.** The *Zhengyi* prints
  scripture and gloss in one stream; the *Questions* argues in paragraphs that
  cross chapter boundaries. Their terms are validated at book level for the
  *Questions*, which the paper discloses, and neither enters Table III.
- **Not exhaustively annotated.** Of 12,702 items, 52.5% are `assert`, 35.3%
  `adopt`, 12.3% `reject`; a compilation quoted 936 times as `adopt` is a record
  of what He Yan chose to reprint, not of what he thought. The gold sample
  oversamples rejections roughly fourfold (20 of 40 against 12.3% of the corpus),
  so its κ is not the corpus κ.
- **Not filtered for doctrinal stakes.** Realia (what a *gu* holds) is 14.0% of
  items and the clustering cannot see a one-character numeral change; that
  counts as agreement, so every divergence figure is a floor.
- **Not a stance-detection benchmark.** The 40 items are a stratified sample with
  rejections oversampled fourfold. κ on them is not κ on the corpus.

## 6. Ethics and licensing

The underlying works are pre-1900 and in the public domain: He Yan (3rd c.),
Xing Bing (11th c.), Zhu Xi (12th c.), Ito Jinsai (1627–1705), Ogyu Sorai
(1666–1728), Liu Baonan (1866). The transcriptions, the annotations, the code and
the derived graphs are released under CC BY 4.0 (`LICENSE-DATA`); code under MIT
(`LICENSE`). Punctuation and page marks added by modern editors may carry rights
in some jurisdictions — cite the edition you use, not this file, for the text.

No personal data. No living authors' text beyond brief quotations inside model
output.
